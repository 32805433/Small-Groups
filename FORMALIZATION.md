# Small presentations: mathematical guide to the formalization

This development proves four existence theorems about finitely presented groups and effective families of presentations. The proof entry point is [SmallPresentation.lean](SmallPresentation.lean); [Assembly.lean](SmallPresentation/Assembly.lean) constructs the two source groups, and [StructuralObligations.lean](SmallPresentation/StructuralObligations.lean) collects their proved construction interfaces. This guide describes the implemented proof and its mathematical sources.

## The four theorems

A presentation `FP n m` has exactly `n` displayed generator slots and `m` displayed relator slots. These counts allow redundant rows and make no assertion of minimality. A word is a finite list of signed generators. A group is **universal** if every finitely presented group admits an injective homomorphism into it. An effective family has a jointly computable relator function in its family and row indices. Undecidability is expressed using `¬ ComputablePred`, not the absence of a classical decidability instance.

All four declarations below are in the namespace `SmallPresentationStatements` in [SmallPresentation.lean](SmallPresentation.lean).

| Declaration | Mathematical statement |
| --- | --- |
| `exists_two_three_universal_with_abelianization_Z` | There is a two-generator, three-relator presentation of a universal group whose abelianization is infinite cyclic and which is normally generated by one displayed generator. |
| `exists_two_four_universal_adian_rabin_family` | There is an effective sequence of two-generator, four-relator presentations with undecidable triviality problem. Every nontrivial group in the sequence is universal. |
| `exists_torsion_free_two_four_with_abelianization` | There is a torsion-free two-generator, four-relator group with undecidable word problem, infinite cyclic abelianization, and a displayed normal generator. |
| `exists_torsion_free_two_five_adian_rabin_family` | There is an effective sequence of two-generator, five-relator presentations with undecidable triviality problem, all of whose groups are torsion-free. |

In the two individual presentations the ordered generators are `(b,s)`, and `s` is the displayed normal generator. The abelianization isomorphism and normal-generation assertion concern the very same presentation witness as the other conclusions. The universal and torsion-free witnesses are separate: a universal group contains finite groups and therefore cannot be torsion-free.

### Word conventions

Conjugation and commutators use `x^g = g⁻¹xg` and `[x,y] = x⁻¹y⁻¹xy`.
An equation used as a presentation row means its left side multiplied by the
inverse of its right side. Indexed products are ordered increasingly.
In the Lean alphabet `Fin n × Bool`, `true` denotes the positive generator and
`false` its inverse. The displayed normal generator is generator `1`, represented
by `[(1, true)]`. The Gordon outputs have ordered generators `(A,B)`.
All homology has integral coefficients; superperfect means `H₁ = H₂ = 0`.

## Construction and dependency architecture

The proof proceeds through two branches with common conversion and packing machinery.

1. Start with a two-generator, thirteen-relator universal group `P`, and calculate its abelianization directly from its displayed rows.
2. For the universal branch, apply two finite mitoses, perfectification, and a normalized universal central extension. This gives a two-generator, 84-relator group `Q` containing `P` and splitting all central extensions.
3. For the torsion-free branch, first replace `P` by a five-generator, nineteen-relator detector HNN extension. It is torsion-free and has undecidable word problem. Apply the same conversion to obtain a two-generator, 216-relator group `Q`, still torsion-free and with undecidable word problem.
4. Pack the universal branch into three rows with modulus `101`, and the torsion-free branch into four rows with modulus `227`.
5. Apply the Gordon attachment to each packed source. Elimination leaves two generators and one more relator, producing the two effective families.

The assembly uses actual injective homomorphisms at every embedding step. In particular, a copy of the seed in a quotient is lifted through a central extension before it is claimed to lie in the final group.

### Seed and undecidability inputs

[Seed.lean](SmallPresentation/Seed.lean) adapts the finite-presentation and universality interfaces of the vendored Universal-Group development. [Seed/Abelianization.lean](SmallPresentation/Seed/Abelianization.lean) proves the infinite-cyclic abelianization from the literal thirteen rows. The seed is chosen from finite coding data; later effectiveness assertions do not require a uniform algorithm that chooses universal embeddings.

The independent existence of a finitely presented group with undecidable word problem is supplied by the fixed seven-generator, fourteen-relator Borisov construction through [UndecidableSeed.lean](SmallPresentation/UndecidableSeed.lean). This prevents a circular argument in which universality alone is treated as an undecidability proof. Once an embedding of an undecidable source is fixed, finite words representing the images of its generators give a computable word substitution and hence a many-one reduction.

The external source code is retained under [UniversalGroup](SmallPresentation/UniversalGroup) and [AdianRabin](SmallPresentation/AdianRabin). The latter shares the former's presentation, HNN, machine, Thue, compression, and simulator foundations; it retains the distinct Gordon and fixed undecidability constructions. The [software provenance and licenses](#software-provenance-and-licenses) section documents this reuse and the local changes. These are source dependencies, not mathematical conclusions accepted as additional axioms.

### Conversion and the actual seed lift

[DetectorModel.lean](SmallPresentation/DetectorModel.lean) realizes the detector as an HNN extension of a product of free groups. Its associated subgroup is the fibre product determined by the original presentation. Membership of a word in that subgroup is detected by a commutator with the stable letter. The normal-form theorem supplies the word-problem reduction, while the HNN torsion theorem supplies torsion-freeness.

[MitosisModel.lean](SmallPresentation/MitosisModel.lean) identifies finite mitosis with two HNN identifications of `J × J`. [MitosisLifting.lean](SmallPresentation/MitosisLifting.lean) gives an algebraic lift through any central extension after two mitoses: images commuting modulo a central kernel have commuting derived images, and a central correction descends to the required lift. This proves the embedding of the original seed into the normalized conversion directly.

Perfectification attaches explicit long code words in a free product with two infinite cyclic factors. The small-cancellation theorem gives the old-factor embedding and torsion preservation; the two dummy rows have an exponent matrix of determinant one, making the resulting group perfect. The normalized central-extension presentation has first two exponent vectors `(1,0)` and `(0,1)` and all remaining vectors zero. The precise recipes and consequences are in [ConversionWords.lean](SmallPresentation/ConversionWords.lean), [PerfectificationConsequences.lean](SmallPresentation/PerfectificationConsequences.lean), [ConversionClosed.lean](SmallPresentation/ConversionClosed.lean), and [ConversionPipeline.lean](SmallPresentation/ConversionPipeline.lean).

For the torsion-free branch, [HomologyConversion.lean](SmallPresentation/HomologyConversion.lean) proves that the actual central kernel is torsion-free. Together with torsion-freeness of the quotient this proves torsion-freeness of the converted group. No rational rank calculation is used as a substitute for this integral assertion.

### Packing and the final displayed rows

[PackingWords.lean](SmallPresentation/PackingWords.lean) defines the signed codes and both final presentations as literal finite words. Put

\[
T=[b,s],\qquad A=bT^{-1},\qquad N=p^2,
\]

using the word conventions above. In terms of the explicitly defined storage words `W_d` and `W`, the three rows are

\[
s^{-1}TsT^{-2},\qquad [A,T^{-1}AT],\qquad T^N W_d^{-1},
\]

and the four rows are

\[
s^{-1}TsT^{-2},\qquad T^{-N}AT^N A^{-1},\qquad
[A,T^{-1}AT],\qquad W.
\]

The code words, substitutions, and ordered products defining `W_d` and `W` are part of the Lean definitions; these abbreviations introduce no generators or relators. The moduli give `N = 10201` and `N = 51529`, respectively.

The proof combines signed-code small cancellation with an exact host model, central relation coordinates, and explicit pullback splittings. [CentralLifting.lean](SmallPresentation/CentralLifting.lean) is the reusable lifting interface. The three-row branch uses [AbsorbedKernel.lean](SmallPresentation/AbsorbedKernel.lean): both kernel inclusions establish a central extension, which suffices to lift the converted source. This branch does not need a calculation of the surviving central kernel's rank.

The four-row branch additionally needs an integral basis and quotient calculation. [PackingRelationBasis.lean](SmallPresentation/PackingRelationBasis.lean), [CentralGraphQuotient.lean](SmallPresentation/CentralGraphQuotient.lean), and [FourPackingKernel.lean](SmallPresentation/FourPackingKernel.lean) describe the orbit relations as bipartite edge relations in a free abelian group. Their quotient remains free abelian. This controls torsion in the actual packed extension.

[PackingConsequences.lean](SmallPresentation/PackingConsequences.lean) verifies that killing `s` collapses each presentation, and constructs the integer character `b ↦ 0`, `s ↦ 1`. The induced mutually inverse maps give the asserted abelianization, not just a surjection onto the integers.

### Gordon families

For a two-generator source with `m` relators, [Gordon.lean](SmallPresentation/Gordon.lean) and [GordonWords.lean](SmallPresentation/GordonWords.lean) define a word-indexed presentation with two generators and `m+1` relators. If the input word is trivial, the displayed equations collapse the output. Otherwise the rank-five free-basis attachment realizes it as an amalgam and embeds the source. [GordonInjection.lean](SmallPresentation/GordonInjection.lean) and [GordonTorsion.lean](SmallPresentation/GordonTorsion.lean) establish these conclusions for the actual presentation.

The exact equivalence is

\[
\mathcal P_w=1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w=1\text{ in the source}.
\]

Computable enumeration of finite words, with a computable right inverse, converts this to a family indexed by the natural numbers. Literal word substitution proves joint computability of its rows. The nontrivial universal outputs contain the universal source; the torsion-free outputs are either torsion-free amalgams or the trivial group.

## Reusable algebraic homology

### Meaning of `H₁` and `H₂`

[Homology.lean](SmallPresentation/Homology.lean) defines `H₁(G)` as the additive form of the abelianization. Its `H₂(G)` is the additive form of the algebraic Schur multiplier. For a free presentation `G = F/R`, the model is

\[
H_2(G)=\frac{R\cap[F,F]}{[F,R]}.
\]

The implementation uses the kernel of the derived central relation extension and proves independence of free presentation. [HopfNaturality.lean](SmallPresentation/HopfNaturality.lean) identifies the actual induced maps under these presentation comparisons. All formulas below use this model. A comparison with Mathlib's chain-complex definition of group homology is not part of this development.

[UniversalCentralExtension.lean](SmallPresentation/UniversalCentralExtension.lean) proves the universal lifting property of `[F,F]/[F,R]` for a perfect quotient, perfectness of this covering group, and its central-extension splitting property. The operational predicate `CentrallyClosed` means that every surjective central extension onto the group has a homomorphic section.

### General formulas and exact sequences

The following interfaces are independent of the two numerical packing instances.

* [SchurQuotientExact.lean](SmallPresentation/SchurQuotientExact.lean) proves the five-term sequence for every normal subgroup `N ◁ B`, with a specified transgression:

  \[
  H_2(B)\longrightarrow H_2(B/N)\longrightarrow N/[B,N]
  \longrightarrow H_1(B)\longrightarrow H_1(B/N)\longrightarrow0.
  \]

* [FreeProductSchur.lean](SmallPresentation/FreeProductSchur.lean) proves the canonical decomposition for an arbitrary indexed family:

  \[
  \bigoplus_a H_2(G_a)\cong H_2\!\left(\mathop{*}_aG_a\right).
  \]

* [KunnethEquivalence.lean](SmallPresentation/KunnethEquivalence.lean) and [Kunneth.lean](SmallPresentation/Kunneth.lean) prove the arbitrary-product degree-two formula

  \[
  H_2(G\times H)\cong H_2(G)\oplus H_2(H)
       \oplus\bigl(H_1(G)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}H_1(H)\bigr),
  \]

  together with the actual inclusion and projection maps, tensor classes, and naturality. The diagonal has coordinates `(x,x,δ(x))`. Its tensor component `δ` is retained and is not assumed to vanish.

* [HNNFamily.lean](SmallPresentation/HNNFamily.lean) treats an arbitrary family of stable letters via the presentation universal property. [HNNCentralLifting.lean](SmallPresentation/HNNCentralLifting.lean) corrects central defects when the degree-one edge boundary has a left inverse. [HNNHomology.lean](SmallPresentation/HNNHomology.lean) proves the two-edge degree-two cokernel formula under projectivity of the vertex and edge abelianizations and this split-boundary hypothesis. These are reusable sufficient hypotheses; an unrestricted graph-of-groups Mayer–Vietoris theorem is not asserted.

For mitosis, the edge maps become

\[
(a,b)\longmapsto(b,-a-b),\qquad
(u,v)\longmapsto(v,-u-v,-\delta(u)).
\]

The first is invertible. The second has cokernel the tensor component, through `(x,y,z) ↦ z-δ(x+y)`. [MitosisSchur.lean](SmallPresentation/MitosisSchur.lean) proves the resulting Schur formula for groups with projective abelianization and proves that two finite mitoses have torsion-free Schur multiplier. This is the homological input needed for torsion-free conversion; the actual seed lift uses the direct central-extension argument above.

## Reusable small-cancellation machinery

### Exact hypotheses and conclusions

Let `F = ∗ₐ Gₐ`, let `(rᵢ)` be an indexed family of nonempty cyclically reduced syllable words, and let `Q = F/⟨⟨rᵢ⟩⟩`. Each syllable is nonidentity, and consecutive syllables, including the cyclic closing pair, lie in different factors.

[SmallCancellationData.lean](SmallPresentation/SmallCancellationData.lean) records an **oriented origin** as a row index, orientation, and cyclic starting position. A semi-initial overlap of length `m` agrees on its first `m−1` syllables and has final syllables in the same factor. It is a piece when the two origins differ. The metric hypothesis is

\[
6m<|r_i|
\]

for every such piece at row `i`. This is the precise formalized version of the strict `C′(1/6)` condition; it includes the final-factor allowance that literal prefix matching alone would miss. Unique oriented triples and row lengths at least 19 are a sufficient criterion, not a restriction on the general theorem.

[RelativeSmallCancellation.lean](SmallPresentation/RelativeSmallCancellation.lean) proves factor injection, quotient-weighted identity balance, and preservation of torsion-freeness. [RelativeRelationModule.lean](SmallPresentation/RelativeRelationModule.lean) proves that the attaching rows form a basis of `N/[F,N]`, where `N = ⟨⟨rᵢ⟩⟩`. [HomologySC.lean](SmallPresentation/HomologySC.lean) proves the full canonical low-degree sequence

\[
0\longrightarrow\bigoplus_aH_2(G_a)
\longrightarrow H_2(Q)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z^{(I)}
\longrightarrow H_1(F)\longrightarrow H_1(Q)\longrightarrow0.
\]

The first map is the sum of the maps induced by the actual factor inclusions. The boundary from `ℤ⁽ᴵ⁾` sends the basis vector indexed by a row to its abelianized value in `F`. Thus the theorem specifies maps and exactness, rather than asserting the existence of unrelated groups with suitable ranks.

### Finite geometry, weighted identities, and torsion

Factor injection is proved independently in [SmallCancellationFactorEmbedding.lean](SmallPresentation/SmallCancellationFactorEmbedding.lean). A nontrivial factor element killed in the quotient yields a marked finite picture. Choose one minimal in its number of new vertices and then its darts. Capping, old-factor replacement, and metric curvature locate an indexed canceling pair away from the marked boundary. Replacing that pair by old factor discs strictly decreases the minimum. The proof retains actual finite ribbon permutations, face equations, vertex classes, and boundary labels through each operation.

Given factor injection, [SmallCancellationIdentityReduction.lean](SmallPresentation/SmallCancellationIdentityReduction.lean) proves the weighted invariant by old-disc erasure and indexed dipole deletion. For an identity expressed as conjugates of old and new relators, each new occurrence contributes its sign together with its conjugator's image in `Q`. For each new row the resulting sum in the free abelian group on `Q` is zero. The stronger invariant retains the information needed for relation modules; scalar exponent balance follows by forgetting the quotient coordinate.

[WeightedIdentitySequences.lean](SmallPresentation/WeightedIdentitySequences.lean) and [RelativeWeightedSplitting.lean](SmallPresentation/RelativeWeightedSplitting.lean) turn this invariant into a split relation-module sequence. [RelationDerivativeExact.lean](SmallPresentation/RelationDerivativeExact.lean) proves the derivative embedding and presentation exact sequence using a normalized section of a free-group surjection. [RelationDerivativeSplitting.lean](SmallPresentation/RelationDerivativeSplitting.lean) identifies the old summand with the separate factor relation modules over quotient cosets. Naturality and the resulting detector prove injectivity of the actual quotient-induced Schur map.

Finally, [RelationTorsionRows.lean](SmallPresentation/RelationTorsionRows.lean) deduces torsion-freeness from the relation-module sequence. A hypothetical nontrivial finite cyclic subgroup acts freely on the factor cosets when the factors are torsion-free. The cyclic difference/norm complex then contradicts the nonzero cyclic cohomology of trivial integer coefficients. This supplies the required torsion theorem without assuming a classification of all finite subgroups.

The general statement that every identity sequence admits a based-Peiffer reduction to old relators is **not claimed**. The implemented geometric theorem proves the quotient-weighted invariant directly. It is sufficient for the relation-module, Schur, and torsion arguments and avoids treating an equality of evaluated products as an equivalence of based sequences.

## Relation to the mathematical sources and scope

The mathematical starting point is the group-theoretic Sections 1–7 of
`small_presentations_and_unrecognizable_four_manifolds.tex`, version 6,
23 October 2026. The supplied source has SHA256
`6eacd269f18d4cfc8799006c80ddef739c537f18ebc1695d56451dca839441f1`.
The formalization retains its displayed presentations and final conclusions.
Its main changes of proof method are algebraic construction of the Hopf/Schur APIs,
direct lifting through two mitoses, finite marked-picture proof of factor injection,
weighted identity balance in place of a general based-Peiffer theorem, and a
cyclic-module torsion obstruction. It does not require a classifying-space attachment
theorem, a general group-cohomology classification of extensions, or a comparison
with topological homology. The manuscript's manifold consequences are outside the
formalized scope.

Some preserved Lean comments refer to `Proofs.md`, the development transcription
of that manuscript. The transcription is omitted from this publication. Its label
`eq:normalized-rows` identifies the normalized rows implemented in
[ConversionWords.lean](SmallPresentation/ConversionWords.lean); `eq:P4-general`
and `eq:three-relator-presentation` identify the final rows implemented in
[PackingWords.lean](SmallPresentation/PackingWords.lean) and displayed above.
The construction interfaces and proof locations in this guide describe the
completed development.

The geometric framework follows Bogley–Pride, but the full collection of their theorems is not claimed as formalized. The scope here is the explicit metric free-product hypotheses and consequences stated above.
